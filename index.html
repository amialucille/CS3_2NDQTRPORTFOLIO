Soup-er Happy Days: Why I LOVE Ramen

There’s something magical about a steaming bowl of ramen on a rainy day. The aroma of the broth, the warmth of the noodles, and the soft-boiled egg resting on top instantly make me feel cozy and at peace. Ramen has been my comfort food since I was young — whether it’s a quiet evening alone with a bowl of miso ramen or a casual night out with friends slurping tonkotsu noodles, it never fails to lift my spirits.

Whenever I’m feeling stressed or exhausted, I crave that rich, flavorful broth and chewy noodles. It’s not just food; it’s a ritual of comfort. In fact, during exam season, I always make sure to have a bowl of ramen — mostly shoyu or buldak — while reviewing my notes. Somehow, the warmth and flavor help calm me down and let me focus, making it easier to lock in all the things I’ve studied.

The different kinds of ramen from around the world also make me curious — each style tells a story, a tradition, a culture, all in a bowl. Ramen is more than just a meal — it’s a moment of warmth, a pause in a busy day, and a reminder that simple things can bring great joy.

Basic Information About Ramen !!

Category Information Notes
Origin Japan (with variations worldwide) Comfort food that has spread globally; each region adapts flavors to local tastes
Main Ingredients Noodles, broth, meat or vegetables Broth styles vary widely: miso (savory), shoyu (soy-based), tonkotsu (rich pork), or seafood-based
Texture Chewy noodles with rich, flavorful broth Soft noodles pair with firm toppings; broth can be creamy or light depending on type
Types Tonkotsu Rich pork bone broth, creamy and hearty
Miso Savory miso paste base, slightly sweet, often with corn or butter
Shoyu Soy sauce-based broth, lighter and salty-sweet
Shio Salt-based, clear and delicate flavors
Buldak-style Korean spicy twist, fiery and sweet
Average Price ₱250 - ₱500 per serving Specialty or fusion ramen may cost more; street versions can be cheaper
